---
title: 'Is AI risk just hype?'
description: 'Some say warnings about AI are marketing by the AI companies. But the warnings come from scientists, defectors and independent investigators, and the companies spend millions fighting the rules a real warning implies.'
---

A common response to warnings about AI goes like this:
AI companies say their products could end the world because it makes them sound powerful.
It attracts investors, it distracts from the harm AI does today, and it invites regulation that only big companies can afford.
So the warnings are marketing, and anyone repeating them is doing the companies' work for free.

Part of this is true.
AI companies do benefit when people believe their products are powerful, and you should not take their word for anything, including their warnings.
But the case for concern does not rest on their word.

## Contents

## The loudest warnings come from people with nothing to sell

The two most cited AI scientists in the world do not work for an AI company.

- **Yoshua Bengio** is the [most cited living scientist](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=kukA0LcAAAAJ) in any field, a Turing Award winner, and a professor at the Université de Montréal. He chairs the [International AI Safety Report](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/), backed by nearly 30 countries, the UN, the OECD and the EU. He [warns](https://yoshuabengio.org/2023/05/22/how-rogue-ais-may-arise/) that "rogue AI may be dangerous for the whole of humanity".
- **Geoffrey Hinton** won the Turing Award and the [2024 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/) for the work that made modern AI possible. He [left Google in 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/05/01/godfather-ai-geoffrey-hinton-quit-google-regrets-lifes-work-bad-actors/) "so that I could talk about the dangers of AI without considering how this impacts Google". He puts the chance that AI wipes out humanity at [10 to 20 percent](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danfitzpatrick/2024/12/29/geoffrey-hintons-prediction-of-human-extinction-at-the-hands-of-ai/).
- **Stuart Russell**, author of the standard AI textbook, [calls it](https://news.berkeley.edu/2023/04/07/stuart-russell-calls-for-new-approach-for-ai-a-civilization-ending-technology/) a potentially "civilization-ending technology".

Hundreds of AI professors signed the [statement on AI risk](https://www.safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-risk).
The [statement calling for a prohibition on superintelligence](https://superintelligence-statement.org/) was signed by Nobel laureates, religious leaders, and politicians from left to right.
None of them profit from making OpenAI look impressive.

## People quit the companies to warn, and pay for it

If the warnings were marketing, they would come from the marketing department.
They come from people walking out the door.

- **Daniel Kokotajlo** left OpenAI in 2024 and [refused to sign a non-disparagement agreement](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2024/5/17/24158478/openai-departures-sam-altman-employees-chatgpt-release), putting most of his family's net worth at stake, so he could speak freely.
- **Jan Leike**, who co-led OpenAI's safety team, [resigned in 2024](https://x.com/janleike/status/1791498184671605209) saying "safety culture and processes have taken a backseat to shiny products".
- **Mrinank Sharma**, head of Anthropic's Safeguards Research team, [resigned in February 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/02/09/anthropic-ai-safety-researcher-warns-of-world-in-peril-in-resignation/), warning that the world is "in peril".
- **Bilal Chughtai** [left Google DeepMind](https://thenextweb.com/news/bilal-chughtai-deepmind-resignation-ai-kill-us-all-warning) in July 2026, warning that AI could kill us all.
- **Jacob Coxon** [resigned from Anthropic](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/09/nx-s1-5962889/anthropic-researcher-resigns-amid-ai-safety-concerns) in September 2026, two months before his shares would vest, to warn against self-improving AI.

In 2026, [1,178 employees](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-anthropic-staff-ask-us-to-pace-frontier-ai-progress) of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Meta asked the US government to help "deliberately pace the frontier".
They were asking someone outside their companies to step in.

## The evidence comes from outside the companies

You don't have to believe what a company says its AI can do.
Capabilities are measured independently.
The research group METR found that the length of tasks AI can complete on its own has [doubled about every seven months](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/) since 2019.

And in the summer of 2026, AI systems did things nobody asked them to do, on computers belonging to other people.
The companies did not discover most of it.

- **Hugging Face**, the victim, [disclosed](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline) that its production systems had been breached. Only then did OpenAI recognise its own agents as the attackers. They had [escaped their test environment](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) and taken administrator access on 41 servers.
- The **UK AI Security Institute**, a government body, [reported](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) 19 unsanctioned actions by agents on the live internet during its own tests, including creating fake identities to manipulate the maintainers of an open-source project.
- **METR** and **Redwood Research** investigated the Hugging Face incident independently ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/), [Redwood](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/research/hugging-face-incident)) and found that nobody had instructed the agents to do it.
- **US state attorneys general** opened investigations. [Fifteen of them](https://forkast.news/15-republican-state-attorneys-general-issue-preservation-demand-to-openai-over-hugging-face-breach/) demanded that OpenAI preserve evidence, and Alabama [subpoenaed the company](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/24/tech/openai-subpoena-hugging-face-attorney-general-alabama).

When Anthropic [disclosed](https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals) that its models had broken into three organisations during testing, it exposed itself to lawsuits.

## The companies fight the rules a real warning implies

If AI companies exaggerated risk to invite regulation that protects them, they would support that regulation.
They fight it.

- In 2024, OpenAI [opposed California's SB 1047](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/21/openais-opposition-to-californias-ai-law-makes-no-sense-says-state-senator/), a bill requiring safety testing of the largest models.
- In 2025, OpenAI's president Greg Brockman and venture firm Andreessen Horowitz helped launch [Leading the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_the_Future), a super PAC network with more than $100 million to fight AI regulation in the 2026 elections.

A sharper version of the hype argument says the companies want rules they write themselves, to lock out competitors.
White House AI adviser David Sacks said this in September 2026, when Amodei, Altman and Musk called for slowing down: they are [building a cartel](https://cdm.press/news/business/2026/09/13/amodei-altman-and-musk-all-want-to-slow-down-ai-david-sacks-says-they-are-building-a-cartel/).

That risk is real, which is why we do not ask for what the companies would want.
We don't propose licenses for AI labs, self-regulation or voluntary commitments.
Our [proposal](/proposal) is an international pause on training the most powerful systems, verified through the [chip supply chain](/building-the-pause-button) and enforced by governments.
It hits the biggest companies hardest, because they are the only ones who can afford frontier training runs.

## Isn't this a distraction from today's harms?

Deepfakes, job losses, surveillance and stolen creative work are real, and they are happening now.
They come from the same place as the bigger risks: a few companies racing to deploy systems faster than anyone can check them, answerable to nobody.

The Hugging Face breach was not a future scenario.
It was a real company whose servers were taken over and a third of whose infrastructure had to be rebuilt.
A pause on the most powerful training runs slows the race that drives both kinds of harm.
We don't think you have to choose.

## Even if you distrust all of them

Suppose you think every scientist is mistaken, every resignation is a career move, and every incident is overblown.
The question is still not whether to believe the companies.
It is whether anyone outside them can set and enforce limits on what they build.

Today, nobody can.
That is what we want to change, and it does not require trusting a single word an AI company says.
