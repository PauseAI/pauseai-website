---
title: 'Is AI risk just hype?'
description: 'Some say warnings about AI are a marketing trick by AI companies. The critique has a true core, but the warnings come from scientists, defectors and independent investigators, and the companies spend millions fighting the rules a real warning implies.'
---

A common response to warnings about AI goes like this:
the AI companies say their products could end the world because it makes them sound powerful.
It attracts investors, it flatters customers, and it distracts from the harms AI causes today.
So the warnings are marketing, and the people repeating them are doing the companies' work for free.

We take this argument seriously, because part of it is true.
But it does not survive a look at who is actually warning, what they risk by doing so, and what the companies do when the warnings turn into rules.

## Contents

## The part that is true

AI companies have used danger as advertising.
In 2019, OpenAI announced GPT-2 was [too dangerous to release](https://openai.com/index/better-language-models/), then released it in full later that year.
Company leaders talk about building something world-changing, and "world-changing" sells.
And when CEOs warn about risks while racing to build the very thing they warn about, scepticism is the healthy reaction.

So we agree on this much: you should not take an AI company's word for anything, including its warnings.
The good news is that you don't have to.

## The loudest warnings come from people with nothing to sell

The two most cited AI scientists in the world do not work for an AI company.

- **Yoshua Bengio** is the [most cited living scientist](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=kukA0LcAAAAJ) in any field, a Turing Award winner, and a professor at the Université de Montréal. He chairs the [International AI Safety Report](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/), backed by nearly 30 countries, the UN, the OECD and the EU. He [warns](https://yoshuabengio.org/2023/05/22/how-rogue-ais-may-arise/) that "rogue AI may be dangerous for the whole of humanity".
- **Geoffrey Hinton** won the Turing Award and the [2024 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/) for the work that made modern AI possible. He [left Google in 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/05/01/godfather-ai-geoffrey-hinton-quit-google-regrets-lifes-work-bad-actors/) "so that I could talk about the dangers of AI without considering how this impacts Google". He puts the chance that AI wipes out humanity at [10 to 20 percent](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danfitzpatrick/2024/12/29/geoffrey-hintons-prediction-of-human-extinction-at-the-hands-of-ai/).
- **Stuart Russell**, who wrote the standard AI textbook used at universities worldwide, [calls it](https://news.berkeley.edu/2023/04/07/stuart-russell-calls-for-new-approach-for-ai-a-civilization-ending-technology/) a potentially "civilization-ending technology".

Hundreds of AI scientists signed the [statement on AI risk](https://www.safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-risk), and Nobel laureates, historians, religious leaders and politicians from left to right signed the [statement calling for a prohibition on superintelligence](https://superintelligence-statement.org/).
Academics get no stock options from OpenAI.

## People quit the companies to warn, and pay for it

If the warnings were marketing, they would come from the marketing department.
Instead, they come from people walking out the door.

- **Daniel Kokotajlo** left OpenAI in 2024 and [refused to sign a non-disparagement agreement](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2024/5/17/24158478/openai-departures-sam-altman-employees-chatgpt-release), putting most of his family's net worth at stake, so he could speak freely.
- **Jan Leike**, who co-led OpenAI's safety team, [resigned in 2024](https://x.com/janleike/status/1791498184671605209) saying "safety culture and processes have taken a backseat to shiny products".
- **Mrinank Sharma**, head of Anthropic's Safeguards Research team, [resigned in February 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/02/09/anthropic-ai-safety-researcher-warns-of-world-in-peril-in-resignation/) warning that the world is "in peril".
- **Bilal Chughtai** [left Google DeepMind](https://thenextweb.com/news/bilal-chughtai-deepmind-resignation-ai-kill-us-all-warning) in July 2026, warning that AI has the potential to kill us all.
- **Jacob Coxon** [resigned from Anthropic](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/09/nx-s1-5962889/anthropic-researcher-resigns-amid-ai-safety-concerns) in September 2026, two months before his shares would vest, to warn against self-improving AI.

In 2026, [1,178 employees](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-anthropic-staff-ask-us-to-pace-frontier-ai-progress) of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Meta asked the US government to help "deliberately pace the frontier".
That is not a sales pitch from their employers.
It is the people building the technology asking someone outside their companies to step in.

## The incidents were found by others

In the summer of 2026, AI systems did things nobody asked them to do, on real computers belonging to real people.
The companies did not discover most of it.

- **Hugging Face**, the victim, [disclosed](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline) that its production systems had been breached. Only then did OpenAI recognise its own agents as the attackers. They had [escaped their test environment](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) and reached administrator access on 41 servers.
- The **UK AI Security Institute**, a government body, [reported](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) 19 unsanctioned actions by agents on the live internet during its own tests, including creating fake identities to manipulate the maintainers of an open-source project.
- **METR** and **Redwood Research**, independent research organisations, investigated the Hugging Face incident ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/), [Redwood](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/research/hugging-face-incident)) and found the behaviour was not instructed.
- **US state attorneys general** opened investigations. [Fifteen of them](https://forkast.news/15-republican-state-attorneys-general-issue-preservation-demand-to-openai-over-hugging-face-breach/) demanded OpenAI preserve evidence, and Alabama [subpoenaed the company](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/24/tech/openai-subpoena-hugging-face-attorney-general-alabama).

When Anthropic [disclosed](https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals) that its own models had broken into three organisations and uploaded a malicious package to a public software repository, it was admitting to legal liability.
Companies do not advertise with subpoenas.

## The companies fight the rules a real warning implies

Here is the strongest test.
If AI companies were exaggerating risk to invite regulation that protects them, they would support that regulation.
They do the opposite.

- In 2024, OpenAI [opposed California's SB 1047](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/21/openais-opposition-to-californias-ai-law-makes-no-sense-says-state-senator/), a modest bill requiring safety testing of the largest models.
- In 2025, OpenAI's president Greg Brockman and venture firm Andreessen Horowitz helped launch [Leading the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_the_Future), a super PAC network with more than $100 million to fight AI regulation in the 2026 elections.

Marketing that companies then spend nine figures to keep from having consequences is not marketing.
It is the companies wanting the attention without the rules.

## What about regulatory capture?

A sharper version of the argument says: the companies don't want _no_ rules, they want rules they write themselves, which lock out competitors.
In September 2026, White House AI adviser David Sacks said exactly this when Amodei, Altman and Musk called for slowing down: they are [building a cartel](https://cdm.press/news/business/2026/09/13/amodei-altman-and-musk-all-want-to-slow-down-ai-david-sacks-says-they-are-building-a-cartel/).

This is a real risk, and it is why we do not ask for what the companies would want.
We do not propose licenses for AI labs, self-regulation, or voluntary commitments.
Our [proposal](/proposal) is an international pause on training the most powerful systems, verified through the [chip supply chain](/building-the-pause-button) and enforced by governments.
That hits the biggest companies hardest, because they are the only ones who can afford frontier training runs.
A cartel does not campaign to stop its own products.

## Even if you distrust all of them

Suppose you think every scientist is mistaken, every resignation is a career move, and every incident is exaggerated.
The question is still not whether to believe the companies.
It is whether anyone outside the companies should be able to set and enforce limits on what they build.

Today, nobody can.
That is the problem we want to fix, and it does not depend on trusting a single word an AI company says.
