description: 'Some say warnings about AI are marketing by the AI companies. But the warnings come from scientists, from people who quit those companies, and from independent investigators. Meanwhile the companies spend millions fighting regulation.'
---

Many people think the warnings about AI are marketing.
The idea is that AI companies say their products could end the world because it makes them sound powerful, which helps them raise money and distracts from the harm AI is doing right now.
Some go further and say the companies want regulation that only they can afford, so the people warning about AI are helping big tech without realising it.

We understand where this comes from.
AI companies do benefit when people think their products are powerful, and you shouldn't take their word for anything.
That includes their warnings.
Luckily, the case for concern doesn't depend on them.

## Contents

## The scientists who warn don't work for AI companies

The two most cited AI researchers in the world are academics.

Yoshua Bengio is the [most cited living scientist](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=kukA0LcAAAAJ) in any field.
He won the Turing Award, teaches at the Université de Montréal, and chairs the [International AI Safety Report](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/), which nearly 30 countries, the UN, the OECD and the EU contribute to.
He [writes](https://yoshuabengio.org/2023/05/22/how-rogue-ais-may-arise/) that "rogue AI may be dangerous for the whole of humanity".

Geoffrey Hinton won the Turing Award and the [2024 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/) for the work that made modern AI possible.
He [left Google in 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/05/01/godfather-ai-geoffrey-hinton-quit-google-regrets-lifes-work-bad-actors/) "so that I could talk about the dangers of AI without considering how this impacts Google".
He estimates the chance that AI wipes out humanity at [10 to 20 percent](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danfitzpatrick/2024/12/29/geoffrey-hintons-prediction-of-human-extinction-at-the-hands-of-ai/).

Stuart Russell, who wrote the textbook most AI students learn from, [calls AI](https://news.berkeley.edu/2023/04/07/stuart-russell-calls-for-new-approach-for-ai-a-civilization-ending-technology/) a potentially "civilization-ending technology".
Hundreds of AI professors signed the [statement on AI risk](https://www.safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-risk), and the [statement calling for a prohibition on superintelligence](https://superintelligence-statement.org/) has signatures from Nobel laureates, religious leaders and politicians from both left and right.

## People leave the companies so they can warn

Many warnings come from people who quit their jobs at AI companies, often at a personal cost.

- **Daniel Kokotajlo** left OpenAI in 2024 and [refused to sign a non-disparagement agreement](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/2024/5/17/24158478/openai-departures-sam-altman-employees-chatgpt-release), even though it put most of his family's net worth at stake.
- **Jan Leike**, who co-led OpenAI's safety team, [resigned in 2024](https://x.com/janleike/status/1791498184671605209) and wrote that "safety culture and processes have taken a backseat to shiny products".
- **Mrinank Sharma** led Anthropic's Safeguards Research team until he [resigned in February 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/conormurray/2026/02/09/anthropic-ai-safety-researcher-warns-of-world-in-peril-in-resignation/), saying the world is "in peril".
- **Bilal Chughtai** [left Google DeepMind](https://thenextweb.com/news/bilal-chughtai-deepmind-resignation-ai-kill-us-all-warning) in July 2026 and warned that AI could kill us all.
- **Jacob Coxon** [resigned from Anthropic](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/09/nx-s1-5962889/anthropic-researcher-resigns-amid-ai-safety-concerns) in September 2026, two months before his shares would have vested, to warn against self-improving AI.

Those who stayed are worried too.
In 2026, [1,178 employees](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-anthropic-staff-ask-us-to-pace-frontier-ai-progress) of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and Meta asked the US government to help "deliberately pace the frontier".

## The evidence comes from outside the companies

You don't need to rely on what a company says its AI can do.
METR, an independent research group, measures how long the tasks are that AI can complete on its own.
That length has [doubled about every seven months](https://metr.org/blog/2025-03-19-measuring-ai-ability-to-complete-long-tasks/) since 2019.

The incidents of summer 2026 were also mostly found by others.
OpenAI agents [escaped their test environment](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) and took over 41 servers at Hugging Face.
OpenAI only realised its own agents were behind it after Hugging Face [disclosed the breach](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline).
[METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/) and [Redwood Research](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/research/hugging-face-incident) investigated and found that nobody had told the agents to do this.
In its own tests, the UK AI Security Institute [counted](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) 19 unsanctioned actions by agents on the live internet, including setting up fake identities to manipulate the maintainers of an open-source project.
[Fifteen US state attorneys general](https://forkast.news/15-republican-state-attorneys-general-issue-preservation-demand-to-openai-over-hugging-face-breach/) demanded that OpenAI preserve evidence, and Alabama [subpoenaed the company](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/24/tech/openai-subpoena-hugging-face-attorney-general-alabama).

Anthropic did report its own incidents: its models [broke into three organisations](https://www.anthropic.com/news/investigating-incidents-cybersecurity-evals) during testing.
That disclosure opens the company up to lawsuits, which is a strange way to advertise.

## The companies fight regulation

If AI companies exaggerated the risks to get regulation that protects them, you would expect them to support that regulation.
In 2024, OpenAI [opposed California's SB 1047](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/21/openais-opposition-to-californias-ai-law-makes-no-sense-says-state-senator/), a bill that required safety testing of the largest models.
In 2025, OpenAI president Greg Brockman and the venture firm Andreessen Horowitz helped launch [Leading the Future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_the_Future), a super PAC network with more than $100 million to spend against AI regulation in the 2026 elections.

Some critics say the companies want rules they write themselves, to keep competitors out.
When Amodei, Altman and Musk called for slowing down in September 2026, White House AI adviser David Sacks accused them of [building a cartel](https://cdm.press/news/business/2026/09/13/amodei-altman-and-musk-all-want-to-slow-down-ai-david-sacks-says-they-are-building-a-cartel/).
We think that risk is real, and it's why we don't ask for licenses for AI labs, self-regulation or voluntary commitments.
Our [proposal](/proposal) is an international pause on training the most powerful systems, verified through the [chip supply chain](/building-the-pause-button) and enforced by governments.
That hits the biggest companies hardest, since they're the only ones who can afford frontier training runs.

## Isn't this a distraction from today's harms?

Deepfakes, job losses and stolen creative work are happening now, and they matter.
They are caused by the same race that worries us, in which a handful of companies ship systems faster than anyone can check them.
Hugging Face had to rebuild a third of its infrastructure after the breach, so the more dangerous kind of harm has already started too.
Slowing down the race at the top helps with both.

## Even if you distrust everyone

Maybe you think the scientists are wrong and the people who quit had other reasons.
Even then, right now nobody outside the AI companies can set and enforce limits on what they build.
We want to change that, and you don't need to believe a word the companies say to agree with us.
